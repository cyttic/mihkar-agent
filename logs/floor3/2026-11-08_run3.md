**Most important goal for this question:** not decided. The table shows what each strategy gains and pays. I ask you to choose below.

**Notes on the table:**
- **Laboratory experiment:** the table says a laboratory experiment usually does not fit a relationship question when the variable cannot be manipulated. Authorship cannot be manipulated here, because the human forms already exist. The row fits only through the rule that tools compared on the same dataset form a laboratory experiment.
- **No experiment label:** the AI versus GitHub comparison is not an experiment. You do not manipulate authorship and you do not assign it at random. The word "experiment" applies only to the AI part, and only if you vary the assistant or the prompt and compare those conditions. That would compare tools with each other. It is a different question from question 3 and would need floor 2.
- **Field study as less common alternative:** it would give you a mechanism (why violations appear). The usual choice gives only counts.

**Accidental limitations to fix in planning:**
- The date cutoff may not separate human code from AI code. Copilot may have had a preview before 21 June 2022, and other AI tools may have existed [from memory — must be verified].
- Published solutions are self-selected. Some authors may be learners. Several solutions may come from one author.
- One form challenge limits the conclusions to that challenge.
- The AI group is not described: which assistants, which prompts, how many forms. If each assistant gets one prompt, the result depends on that wording.
- Automated checkers find only part of the accessibility problems [my inference]. Name the checker and its version. Run it with the same settings on both groups.
- Some GitHub solutions may not build or render. Decide in advance how to handle them.

**Possible combination:** your definitions already combine rows 1 and 2. The sample study (neutral) supplies real human solutions. The laboratory-experiment part (contrived) controls how the AI forms are produced. The price is that the two groups come from different settings. The comparison stays observational and is confounded by who publishes and how each form was produced [my inference].

**What you must check yourselves:**

- Which AI assistants can you use, and under what terms?
- Does the ethics committee need to review the use of public repositories? Do not store usernames or other identifying details (constitution rule 30).

I did not search in this chat, so cite them only after you read them yourselves [from memory — must be verified]. The sources I know lean toward English-language and Western research.
